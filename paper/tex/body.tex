\section{Results}

\subsection{The corpus once its records are made comparable}
\label{sec:index}

We resolved identity across 54 public databases at the level of the crystal
structure, matching each incoming record first on a cross-database identifier the
source is entitled to assert and, failing that, on a structural fingerprint,
never on the chemical formula (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}a; Methods). Records
that match nothing are admitted rather than discarded, and every value retains
the database, the native record and the method behind it.

The corpus so assembled holds 13{,}543{,}277 distinct crystal structures, of
which 357{,}127 are experimental and 13{,}186{,}150 are computational candidates.
On that pool sit 121{,}620{,}776 property values spanning 166 properties,
1{,}198{,}897 stored spectra and 17{,}327{,}895 references recording which
database contains which crystal.

The contributors are extremely unequal (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}b). Their
contributions span 5.7 orders of magnitude; the largest supplies half of all
values and the five largest supply four fifths, while 30 of the 54 supply fewer
than $10^5$ each. The asymmetry is not a curiosity of bookkeeping, because the
two ends of the distribution do different work. The large general-purpose
databases determine how much of the pool carries any property at all; the small
specialised libraries determine how many distinct properties exist to carry, and
the single most property-diverse contributor supplies 61 of the 166 properties
while the largest supplies 10.

Assembling the corpus enlarged it (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}c). The
deduplicated starting pool held 8{,}225{,}138 structures; admitting the records
that matched nothing added 5{,}318{,}139 more, a growth of 64.7\%. Of the
17{,}327{,}895 references, 6{,}784{,}564 were resolved by record identifier,
5{,}126{,}030 by structural fingerprint alone, and the remainder record a
database's own entry (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}d). The two matched routes are
not interchangeable. Identifier matching is exact but available only where a
database publishes a cross-reference it owns, which is why it dominates among the
general-purpose repositories and is nearly absent among the framework libraries;
fingerprint matching is available everywhere but has a domain of validity.

That domain has an edge, and it is worth stating before any conclusion is drawn
from the corpus. A bonding-graph fingerprint identifies a crystal by its
connectivity and its primitive composition, which is the right invariant for
crystals that differ in either. Enumerated framework libraries do not: they are
generated by decorating a small number of parent topologies, so sibling entries
are identical in everything the fingerprint reads. Left untreated, the
fingerprint path collapses more than $10^5$ distinct hypothetical zeolite
frameworks onto a few hundred pool materials and discards a comparable number of
hypothetical metal--organic frameworks as duplicates of one another. We detect
this at the level of the source rather than the record, and admit such libraries
under a composite key that adds the cell volume per site, an invariant the graph
hash ignores (Supplementary Note~\ref{si-note:fingerprint}). An audit that checks
each source independently for load integrity, staging yield and field coverage
reports no discrepancies over the completed database
(Supplementary Note~\ref{si-note:audit}).

A structure admitted from one database becomes recognisable to every database
that follows, because its fingerprint and identifiers join the matching substrate
as it is admitted. The corpus therefore does not merely accumulate: the criterion
by which it recognises the next record is itself a product of the records already
ingested, and it is by this mechanism that the database keeps itself current as
sources publish new versions and new sources appear. The effect is measurable. Of
the 11{,}910{,}594 references resolved by matching, 1{,}898{,}275, or 15.9\%,
land on a crystal that was absent from the starting pool and was admitted during
assembly (Fig.~\ref{fig:redundancy}c). Those links do not exist in a construction
that matches every database against a fixed reference.

The effect is ordered by ingestion time and by chemical distance from the
starting pool. AFLOW, ingested about ninety minutes after Alexandria contributed
1{,}514{,}488 structures, resolved 47.7\% of its 1{,}401{,}895 matches onto
crystals admitted during assembly. The JARVIS tight-binding set, whose fitting
geometries are deliberately non-equilibrium and largely absent from the starting
pool, reached 67.6\%. The framework libraries show the effect in its purest form:
CoRE MOF, ingested last, resolved 99.5\% of its matches onto frameworks
contributed by four earlier libraries in the same run. Databases whose chemistry
the starting pool already covered show the converse, at or near zero. The
practical consequence is that the marginal value of adding a database is not
confined to the values it brings: CoRE MOF contributed 155{,}488 experimental
porosity measurements, and the reason 99.5\% of them landed on an existing
crystal rather than creating an isolated set of new ones is that earlier
libraries had already put those frameworks in the pool. Assembled against a
frozen reference, the same measurements would have arrived as an unconnected
island.

\subsection{Redundancy between databases, and whether the duplicates agree}
\label{sec:redundancy}

How much of the public corpus is the same crystal counted more than once is a
question that only a structurally resolved union can answer, and the answer is
that a substantial fraction is. Of the 12{,}827{,}418 structures carrying at
least one database reference, 1{,}190{,}447 carry two or more
(Fig.~\ref{fig:redundancy}a). The distribution falls steeply and reaches far:
870{,}439 crystals are described by exactly two databases, 158{,}623 by three,
2{,}058 by thirteen and one by 24. Redundancy is not spread evenly over
chemistry; it is concentrated where the field has concentrated its effort. The
largest single overlap holds 451{,}178 shared crystals, and the ten largest
overlaps are all between general-purpose density functional theory repositories
(Fig.~\ref{fig:redundancy}b). These are the numbers a federated query cannot
report and a formula-level join would misstate in both directions, and they bound
what any cross-database evaluation can claim: for the largest overlapping pairs,
a model trained on one repository and tested on another is tested on hundreds of
thousands of crystals it has already seen, unless the split is taken over
structures.

Redundancy is only interesting if the redundant copies can be compared, and they
can be compared only because every value kept the method that produced it. Where
two databases report the same quantity for the same crystal under comparable
methods, we took the median of each database's own values and the spread across
databases (Fig.~\ref{fig:redundancy}d--f;
Supplementary Note~\ref{si-note:agreement}). The comparison is possible on a
large sample: 870{,}683 crystals carry a formation energy from two or more
databases, 452{,}584 a band gap, 376{,}813 a hull distance, 145{,}353 a
magnetisation and 15{,}275 a bulk modulus. Concordance falls as the quantity
becomes harder to compute. Within 50~meV, 75.9\% of shared band gaps agree;
within 50~meV\,atom$^{-1}$, 64.1\% of hull distances and 60.5\% of formation
energies do; within 5~GPa, 44.2\% of bulk moduli and 49.4\% of shear moduli do.

The tail matters more than the median. The tenth of shared formation energies
that agree least differ by more than 248~meV\,atom$^{-1}$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:redundancy}e), which is several times the hull-distance margins
conventionally used to call a compound stable
\cite{sun2016thermodynamic,bartel2020critical}. Whether a candidate passes such a
filter can therefore depend on which database supplied its energy, and in a
corpus without structural identity that dependence is not merely unquantified but
unobservable.

Resolving the comparison by pair separates two regimes that a single agreement
figure would merge (Fig.~\ref{fig:redundancy}f). Some pairs agree almost exactly.
Two of the largest repositories share 138{,}961 crystals whose band gaps have a
median absolute difference of exactly zero and agree within tolerance for 93.3\%
of them. That is not independent confirmation; it is the signature of shared
provenance, two datasets carrying one underlying calculation, and reading it as
corroboration would invert its meaning. Other pairs disagree systematically. For
formation energy the median signed difference between the Materials Project and
JARVIS-DFT is $+70.7$~meV\,atom$^{-1}$ against a median absolute difference of
92.7~meV\,atom$^{-1}$, so nearly all of the disagreement carries one sign; the
corresponding figures are $+171$ against 177~meV\,atom$^{-1}$ for the Materials
Project against AFLOW. Pearson correlations across these pairs remain between
0.85 and 0.99, so what separates the databases is an offset and a scale rather
than noise. This is precisely the structure that merging columns by name would
absorb without trace, and precisely the structure a model trained on the merged
column would learn as if it were physics. Method strings make the largest offsets
interpretable rather than merely visible: the widest gap among the major pairs,
$+150$ against 155~meV\,atom$^{-1}$, is between a density functional and a
tight-binding dataset, and is legible as such only because every value in the
latter carries a method string that says so.

Holding several databases against one another also exposes individual values that
no single-source check would question. Among the shared elastic moduli, 380 bulk
moduli published by one repository and 316 published by another lie outside any
physically plausible range, being negative or above 1000~GPa, with the largest
magnitude reaching $4.7\times10^{11}$~GPa. These are unconverged elastic-tensor
fits published as they stand. In isolation each is a number in a column; set
against a partner value on the same crystal, each is immediately identifiable.

\subsection{Coverage follows what a property costs, not what it is worth}
\label{sec:coverage}

The assembled corpus makes its own principal deficiency measurable. Of the 166
properties in the registry, 151 carry at least one value; treating the pool and
those 151 properties as a matrix gives 2{,}045{,}034{,}827 cells, of which
95{,}128{,}921 are occupied, so the matrix is 4.65\% full
(Fig.~\ref{fig:landscape}a). Occupancy is extremely uneven: nine properties cover
more than 40\% of the pool, while 95 of the 151 cover less than 1\% and 58 cover
less than 0.1\% (Supplementary Table~\ref{si-tab:properties}). Half of all
crystals carry at most six of the 151 properties (Fig.~\ref{fig:landscape}c).

  \label{fig:landscape}

The gradient does not follow scientific importance. It follows price
(Fig.~\ref{fig:landscape}b). Grouping 69 general-purpose properties, excluding
those whose domain is one material family, by the calculation each requires
(Supplementary Note~\ref{si-note:tiers}), 19 are read off a single
self-consistent calculation a database performs anyway: total and formation
energies, hull distance, band gaps and edges, magnetisation, the density of
states at the Fermi level. Their median coverage is 4.56\% of the pool and eight
exceed 10\%. The remaining 50 require a calculation of their own, from an extra
slab or strained cell through linear response to anharmonic phonons, transport
integrals and hybrid functionals. Their median coverage is 0.107\%, lower by a
factor of 43, and \emph{not one of the fifty reaches 10\%}. The best covered
among them is the electronic dielectric constant at 1.67\%, followed by the
Voigt--Reuss--Hill bulk modulus at 1.45\%.

Within that second group the ordering is set by whether a dedicated campaign ever
existed rather than by cost alone. Linear-response properties, the more expensive
of the remaining tiers, have a higher median coverage (1.06\%) than the
properties reachable from a few extra self-consistent runs (0.029\%), because the
elastic and dielectric tensors were the explicit target of high-throughput
efforts \cite{dejong2015charting,petousis2017high} while quantities such as the
work function were not. Coverage in this corpus therefore records where the
community chose to spend computer time, and that map is legible only once the
databases sit on one structure pool.

The same stratification governs non-scalar data and provenance. The curve store
holds 1{,}198{,}897 spectra across ten types but reaches only 298{,}662 crystals,
2.2\% of the pool (Fig.~\ref{fig:landscape}e). And only 64 of the 151 populated
properties are reported by two or more databases; the other 87 have a single
supplier and therefore no independent check of any kind. Of the 95{,}128{,}921
occupied cells, 3.9\% carry values from two or more sources, which is the exact
extent of the corpus on which the comparison of Section~\ref{sec:redundancy} is
possible.

\subsection{Learning the properties that were never computed}
\label{sec:completion}

The coverage gradient states a problem the corpus is now equipped to attack.
Nearly ten million crystals carry a formation energy and 7.8 million a band gap;
196{,}615 carry a bulk modulus and 26{,}507 a lattice thermal conductivity. The
missing entries are not missing because they are uninteresting. They are missing
because obtaining them costs between one and four orders of magnitude more than
the calculation that produced the entries that are present, and filling the
matrix by computation would require an effort no single group can supply.
Learning it instead requires three things a single database does not provide, and
that the assembled corpus does: deduplicated structures, so that a train--test
split falls on distinct crystals rather than on records; labels that retain the
quantity they denote; and, on the subset with two independent sources, a
measurement of the noise floor a prediction should be compared against.

A census of the database establishes how much is actually trainable
(Fig.~\ref{fig:completion}a). The unit that matters is not the property but the
property at one level of theory, because a name such as band gap covers several
different physical quantities that a model cannot distinguish from structure
alone. Split that way, the 151 populated properties yield 497 candidate tasks. Of
these, 161 regression tasks have at least $10^4$ crystals to train on, 97 have at
least $10^5$, and 13 have at least $10^6$. Constructing the training sets under a
default policy that keeps the largest level of theory per property produced 162
sets covering 116 properties and 93{,}462{,}873 labels, each with a
chemistry-aware train, validation and test split.

Three properties of the corpus determine how those labels must be handled, and
each of them is a direct consequence of the integration reported above. The first
is that a property name is not a label definition (Fig.~\ref{fig:completion}b).
Band gap carries 13 distinct levels of theory, of which the semilocal group holds
81\% and a tight-binding fit a further 8\%; the systematic offsets between them
are of order 1~eV, which a model cannot infer from the structure and would
instead learn as a proxy for which database supplied the row. The second is that
some quantities are condition-dependent rather than multiply determined: carbon
dioxide uptake is reported at four distinct grand-canonical conditions and
hydrogen uptake at five temperature and pressure combinations, so a material
legitimately carries several different correct values and taking their median
produces a number that is not any physical quantity. The third is the
cross-database disagreement of Section~\ref{sec:redundancy}, which here becomes
an operational quantity rather than an observation: across the 162 training sets,
5{,}831{,}192 crystals carry labels from two or more databases and 519{,}599 of
them disagree by more than a quarter of the target's standard deviation. Those
spreads are retained alongside the resolved label so that they can weight the
loss, rather than being averaged away.

Splitting the data is a fourth constraint of the same kind. The same compound
recurs in the pool under different accession numbers, so a random split places
identical chemistry in training and test, and the resulting scores are optimistic
in exactly the regime that matters, since the crystals a completion is wanted for
are the ones whose chemistry the training set never saw. Holding out whole
chemical systems is the right default, but it degenerates on the enumerated
libraries, where an entire task can occupy a single chemical system: a
chemistry-level holdout leaves the validation and test folds of such a task
empty, which happened for 23 of the 162 sets before the split rule was made to
fall back and to record which level it used.

Finally, the completion has a domain (Fig.~\ref{fig:completion}c,d). Carbon
dioxide uptake is a grand-canonical adsorption quantity: it is not zero for a
dense intermetallic, it is undefined, because the simulation cell has nowhere to
insert a molecule. Of the 162 training sets, 63 draw more than 90\% of their
labels from a single material family, and the largest declared domains are
pool-wide (78 tasks), porous (25), ion-conducting (22) and gapped (15). Enforcing
those domains changes the size of the completion problem substantially: 105 of
the 162 tasks are defined on less than half the pool, and the number of
predictions that lie inside a declared domain is 871{,}097{,}089 rather than the
2.19 billion that asking every task about every crystal would produce, a factor
of 2.5. The difference is not a loss of coverage. It is the removal of numbers
that would have looked like data.

One dependency in that structure is worth stating because it closes the loop with
Section~\ref{sec:index}. Seventeen of the tasks are gated on a property the database
does not yet hold: whether a crystal has a gap, or whether it is magnetically
ordered, is unknown for 75{,}674{,}942 structure-task pairs, so their
admissibility can only be decided once the corresponding tier-1 models have run.
The completion therefore proceeds in stages, and each stage enlarges the domain
over which the next one is defined, in the same way that each admitted structure
enlarged the substrate against which the next database was matched.

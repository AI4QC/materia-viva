\section{Discussion}

Resolving identity at the level of the crystal changes what a materials database
is for. A repository answers questions about its own contents; a structurally
resolved union answers questions about the field. How many distinct crystals are
actually known, as opposed to how many records exist. Which groups have already
computed a given compound, and whether they agreed. Whether a training set and a
test set drawn from two repositories share the same crystals. None of these is a
property of any member, and none can be recovered afterwards from a corpus
assembled by concatenation.

The most consequential finding is the one that a single repository is
structurally incapable of producing. Independent databases reporting the same
formation energy for the same crystal agree within 50~meV\,atom$^{-1}$ only 60.5\%
of the time, and the least concordant tenth differ by margins several times the
thresholds on which stability screening turns. Because the discrepancy is a
systematic offset rather than scatter, it does not average away with sample size,
and a model trained on a corpus that merged the contributing columns would learn
the offset as though it were physics. The remedy is not to choose a preferred
provider but to keep the provenance, which costs one column per value and makes
the disagreement an observable rather than a hazard. The complementary caution is
equally practical: a pair of databases agreeing to the last decimal is usually
carrying one calculation twice, so agreement between repositories should be read
as evidence of shared provenance until shown otherwise.

The second finding concerns what the corpus is missing. Coverage of the property
space follows the price of the calculation, not the value of the answer. Not one
of the 50 general-purpose properties requiring a calculation of its own reaches a
tenth of the pool, and within that group coverage tracks whether a dedicated
high-throughput campaign happened to exist rather than the cost itself. The
resulting landscape is therefore a record of where the community spent computer
time, which is a different thing from a record of what is worth knowing.

That landscape is also a well-posed learning problem, and the corpus supplies
what such a problem needs: 497 candidate tasks once each property name is split
by level of theory, 162 of them realised as training sets over 93 million labels,
deduplicated structures to split on, labels that retain the quantity they denote
and, on the entries with two independent sources, a measurement of the floor
below which a prediction cannot be distinguished from the disagreement of its own
training data. Section~\ref{sec:completion} also shows how easily that problem is
posed wrongly. Roughly two thirds of the tasks are defined on less than half the
pool, and asking each of them about every crystal would generate 2.19 billion
numbers of which more than half would be undefined rather than merely uncertain.
Enforcing the domains is not a loss of coverage; it is the difference between a
completed property layer and a plausible-looking one.

The construction has a property worth separating from the results it produced.
Admission and matching are the same mechanism seen from two sides: a record that
matches nothing is not an error to be logged but the pool's next member, and once
admitted it becomes part of the criterion by which the next record is judged.
Over the full assembly, 15.9\% of matches resolved onto crystals that assembly
itself had added, approaching unity for chemistries the starting pool did not
cover. The same relation reappears one level up in the completion stage, where
seventeen tasks can only be gated once earlier models have supplied the property
their domain is defined by. A database built this way does not have a final state:
each round of ingestion enlarges what the next round can recognise, and each
round of prediction enlarges the domain over which the next round is defined.

Several limitations bound these conclusions. Structural fingerprinting has a
domain of validity, and enumerated framework libraries fall outside it; the
composite key rescues the two families we encountered but is a remedy for a known
failure rather than a general solution. Agreement can be measured only where two
providers report the same quantity under comparable methods, which the method
strings make checkable but which holds for a minority of the occupied matrix, so
the disagreement reported here is measured on the part of the corpus most likely
to be well behaved. The applicability gate is currently structural rather than
geometric: a porous domain is enforced through family labels rather than through
a computed pore-limiting diameter, which is the stricter test and remains to be
run over the whole pool. Licence fragmentation persists downstream, since four
sources contributing 26.0\% of all values cannot be redistributed, so a public
artifact and a locally built one are not the same object and any published
statistic has to say which it describes. And no value here was recomputed. Every
number is as its source reported it, and integration inherits whatever errors
those sources contain; what it adds is the ability to see when two of them
disagree.

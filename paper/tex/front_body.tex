\section{Introduction}

Materials discovery follows two routes. Forward screening searches the crystals
already known for one that has the property wanted. Inverse design generates a
crystal to order, and reports as its main figure of merit how many of its
products are new. Each route is usually described as bounded by something of its
own: forward screening by how many crystals are known, inverse design by how good
the generator is. Neither bound is where it is placed. Both routes rest on one
question, asked of every crystal they touch: is this structure already known,
and is this property already known for it? No public database can answer that
question on its own.

The reason is that the databases share no key at the level of the crystal. Public
repositories hold millions of computed structures
\cite{jain2013commentary,saal2013materials,curtarolo2012aflow,schmidt2022dataset,%
kirklin2015oqmd,choudhary2020jarvis,draxl2019nomad,talirz2020materials}, and
generative models and universal interatomic potentials are trained on them
\cite{xie2022crystal,zeni2025generative,merchant2023scaling,chen2022universal,%
deng2023chgnet,batatia2023foundation,yang2024mattersim,wood2025uma}. But the
same arrangement of atoms carries a different accession number in every database
that holds it, and the chemical formula cannot serve as a key because polymorphs
share one. So the union cannot be deduplicated, values from different providers
cannot be placed on the same object, and three things stay unmeasured: how much
of the record is one crystal counted several times, whether two sources that
computed the same quantity agree, and which properties are known for which
crystals. OPTIMADE gave the databases a common query interface
\cite{andersen2021optimade,evans2024developments} and LeMaterial published a
deduplicated union of several large ones \cite{ramlaoui2025lemat}. Neither
delivers the property layer, because two databases publishing a column called
band gap are often not reporting the same physical quantity, and a union that
merges by column name averages a semilocal, a hybrid and a measured value into
one number.

That absence bounds each route in a specific way. A forward screen can rank
crystals that nobody has computed, since that is what a surrogate model is for.
What it cannot do is reach beyond the pool it was given or trust its labels more
than the sources that produced them. A campaign screens whichever database it
loaded and trains on that database's values, so the limit of forward screening
is set by the size of the pool and the consistency of the labels, and both come
from the record. Inverse design reports novelty against a reference assembled
from a few of the largest repositories
\cite{ramlaoui2025lemat,betala2025lematgenbench}, so the limit of what it may
call new is set by the reference and by the criterion used to match against it,
and both again come from the record. Three recent assessments make the point. A
survey of one campaign's 384{,}870 stable predictions found them to be
modifications of known materials \cite{cheetham2024artificial}. The flagship
compound of a generative model was later identified as a disordered phase
reported in 1972 and present in the model's own training set
\cite{juelsholt2026continued}. And an ion-exchange baseline built from known
compounds outperformed four generative techniques \cite{szymanski2025baselines}.
In each case the record already contained what was reported as new.

Here we build the record that both routes need. Identity is resolved across 55
databases at the level of the crystal structure. Every value keeps the source,
the native record and the method that produced it, so that several sources
speaking about one crystal stay separately audible. Licence boundaries are
enforced in the pipeline. Admission and recognition are one operation: a record
that matches nothing becomes the corpus's next member, and its fingerprint joins
the criterion applied to every record after it, so the corpus grows as it
ingests rather than being rebuilt. The Results first describe the record, then
what it shows for forward screening, then what it shows for inverse design.

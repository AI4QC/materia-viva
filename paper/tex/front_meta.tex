\title[A self-evolving materials database]{A self-evolving materials database
that unifies 54 public sources into one searchable space of 13.5 million
crystals}

% ------------------------------------------------------------- ABSTRACT
\abstract{Crystal structures are not scarce. What the field lacks is a materials
space that can be searched, compared and traced back to its source in one place,
and assembling one from the public databases consumes much of the effort that
should go into screening. Those databases share no key at the level of the object
they describe, so one crystal appears in each under a different accession number,
format and licence, and a union merged once ages immediately because every source
keeps growing. Here we build that space. Identity is resolved at the level of the
crystal across 54 databases, every value keeps the source, record and method that
produced it, and admission and recognition are made one operation: a record
matching nothing becomes the corpus's next member, and its fingerprint at once
enlarges the criterion by which the following record is judged, so the database
evolves as it ingests rather than being rebuilt. Assembly enlarged the
deduplicated pool by 64.7\%, and 15.9\% of all cross-source matches land on a
crystal it had itself added, rising to 99.5\% for the source ingested last. The
result holds 13.5 million distinct crystals and 121.6 million values over 166
properties, and answers questions no member source can. Redundancy proves large
and concentrated, with 1{,}190{,}447 crystals described by two or more sources,
and those sources often disagree: of 870{,}683 crystals carrying a formation
energy from more than one, only 60.5\% agree within 50~meV\,atom$^{-1}$, and the
disagreement is a systematic offset rather than scatter. Coverage follows
computational cost rather than scientific value, no property needing a
calculation of its own reaching a tenth of the crystals, and that gap becomes a
tractable learning problem for which the corpus supplies 497 bounded tasks.}

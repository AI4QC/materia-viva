\title[A self-evolving materials database]{A self-evolving materials database of
13.5 million crystals reveals systematic disagreement between its sources}

% ------------------------------------------------------------- ABSTRACT
\abstract{Materials discovery now draws on the union of the public crystal
databases, yet that union has never been assembled. The databases describe
overlapping sets of crystals under unrelated identifiers, so neither the
duplication within the corpus nor the consistency between its contributors can be
measured, and a union merged once ages immediately because the sources keep
growing. Here we resolve identity at the level of the crystal across 54
databases, keep every value attached to its source, record and method, and make
admission and recognition one operation: a record matching nothing becomes the
corpus's next member, and its structural fingerprint at once enlarges the
criterion by which the following record is judged, so the database evolves as it
ingests rather than being rebuilt. Assembly enlarged the deduplicated pool by
64.7\%, and 15.9\% of all cross-database matches land on a crystal it had itself
added, rising to 99.5\% for the source ingested last. Among the resulting 13.5
million crystals and 121.6 million values, 1{,}190{,}447 crystals are described
by two or more sources, and those sources disagree: of 870{,}683 crystals with a
formation energy from more than one, only 60.5\% agree within
50~meV\,atom$^{-1}$, and the least concordant tenth differ by several times the
margin used to judge a compound stable. The disagreement is a systematic offset rather than scatter, and much
apparent agreement elsewhere is shared provenance rather than independent
confirmation. Coverage follows computational cost rather than scientific value,
no property needing a calculation of its own reaching a tenth of the crystals.
The corpus yields 497 learnable tasks, each bounded by the domain its property is
defined on, and the same self-extending relation reappears among them.}
